## Predict two subtractions

Chapter 21 ends with equally normalized named-parameter gradients. Give the
first parameter leaf this tiny state:

$$
\theta_0=[1,-2],
\qquad
g_1=[0.2,-0.4].
$$

Use $\eta=0.1$, $\beta_1=\beta_2=0.5$, $\varepsilon=0.1$, and
$\lambda=0.1$. Predict the adaptive delta and the decay delta separately.

The zero-started moments are $m_1=[0.1,-0.2]$ and
$v_1=[0.02,0.08]$. Both correction denominators equal $0.5$, giving
$\hat m_1=[0.2,-0.4]$ and $\hat v_1=[0.04,0.16]$. Therefore:

$$
\eta\frac{\hat m_1}{\sqrt{\hat v_1}+\varepsilon}
\approx[0.066667,-0.08],
\qquad
\eta\lambda\theta_0=[0.01,-0.02].
$$

Subtract both from the old value to predict
$\theta_1\approx[0.923333,-1.9]$. The signs matter: decaying the negative
coordinate subtracts a negative delta, moving it toward zero.

## Correct moments, then bypass them with decay

Update the elementwise first and second raw moments:

$$
m_t=\beta_1m_{t-1}+(1-\beta_1)g_t,
\qquad
v_t=\beta_2v_{t-1}+(1-\beta_2)g_t^2.
$$

The first moment carries recent gradient direction forward, which is the
momentum intuition: consistent directions reinforce one another while a sudden
reversal is softened. The second raw moment tracks recent squared magnitude,
so coordinates with persistently large gradients receive smaller adaptive
steps.

Their zero initialization suppresses early values. Correct that missing mass:

$$
\hat m_t=\frac{m_t}{1-\beta_1^t},
\qquad
\hat v_t=\frac{v_t}{1-\beta_2^t}.
$$

Now apply the chapter's exact shared formula:

$$
\hat m_t=\frac{m_t}{1-\beta_1^t},\quad \hat v_t=\frac{v_t}{1-\beta_2^t},\quad \theta_t=(1-\eta\lambda)\theta_{t-1}-\eta\frac{\hat m_t}{\sqrt{\hat v_t}+\varepsilon}
$$

Equivalently, subtract the adaptive delta and
$\eta\lambda\theta_{t-1}$ as two explicit terms. Decoupling means the second
term bypasses $g_t$, $m_t$, and $v_t$; it does not mean the final replacement
ignores the adaptive branch.

The group map is an explicit partition of the stable parameter-name set: its
decay and no-decay sets are disjoint, and their union contains every name. This
fixture decays `decoder.output.weight` but excludes `decoder.norm.scale`.
Decoder weight matrices are commonly shrinkage targets, while normalization
scales and biases are commonly excluded so decay does not directly pull those
calibration terms toward zero. A different model may choose another documented
partition.

## Keep step state and parameter state distinct

- $\theta_{t-1}$ is one named parameter tensor before step $t$.
- $g_t$ is its accumulated token-mean loss gradient.
- $m_t$ and $v_t$ are name-keyed moving estimates with the same shape.
- $\beta_1$ and $\beta_2$ retain past first and second raw moments, respectively.
- $\hat m_t$ and $\hat v_t$ correct their early-step zero bias.
- $\eta>0$ is the learning rate; $\lambda\geq0$ is decay.
- $\varepsilon>0$ stabilizes the adaptive denominator after its square root.
- $\theta_t$ is a fresh trainable leaf installed only after the whole set succeeds.

On the fresh probe, $m_0=v_0=0$ and $g_1=0$, so both new moments and the
adaptive delta are zero. A decay-group parameter still shrinks through
$\eta\lambda\theta_0$, while a no-decay parameter remains unchanged. After an
earlier nonzero gradient, $g_t=0$ only removes the new contribution: stored
moments decay but can still produce a nonzero adaptive update. The group
controls decay, not moment memory.

## From one word gradient to AdamW-trained decoders

[Bengio et al., *A Neural Probabilistic Language Model*](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume3/bengio03a/bengio03a.pdf)
provide the earlier language-model evidence. Bengio et al.'s neural language
model performs a direct stochastic parameter update after presenting one
training word and its context. One shared learning rate moves every coordinate
from the current gradient, without memory of earlier gradient magnitudes. In
loss-minimization notation, the core move is simply:

$$
\theta\leftarrow\theta-\eta g.
$$

Momentum first carries a decaying velocity across successive language-model
gradients. Adam then keeps exponential first and second raw gradient moments
and corrects their early zero-initialization bias; if an $L_2$ term is coupled
into its gradient, that parameter-proportional term enters both moving
estimates. AdamW instead moves the shrinkage term outside the gradient entering
those adaptive moments.

In compact loss-minimization notation, momentum uses
$u_t=\mu u_{t-1}+g_t$ and $\theta_t=\theta_{t-1}-\eta u_t$. Coupled $L_2$ feeds
$g_t+\lambda\theta_{t-1}$ into Adam's moments; AdamW leaves $g_t$ as the moment
input and applies shrinkage separately. Here $u_t$ is the retained update
velocity and $0\leq\mu\lt1$ is its retention rate.

[Kingma and Ba, *Adam: A Method for Stochastic Optimization*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.6980)
support the adaptive stage: Kingma and Ba's Adam keeps exponential first and
second raw gradient moments and corrects their early zero-initialization bias.
They divide by
$1-\beta_1^t$ and $1-\beta_2^t$ because zero initialization biases early
estimates toward zero. Adam is a general optimizer, not an LLM architecture.

[Loshchilov and Hutter, *Decoupled Weight Decay Regularization*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.05101)
show that an $L_2$ penalty mixed into the loss gradient is not equivalent to
weight decay for an adaptive optimizer. Loshchilov and Hutter's AdamW then
moves parameter-proportional decay outside the gradient entering those
adaptive moments.

LLaMA documents AdamW in pretraining decoder language models from $7$B to $65$B
parameters. The course's optimizer now converts token-mean autograd gradients
into fresh named parameter leaves; schedules, clipping, mixed precision, and
distributed state remain later pipeline concerns.

That progression—from direct next-word gradients to adaptive, decoupled
updates used in decoder pretraining—is why AdamW belongs on the road to modern
LLMs.

[Touvron et al., *LLaMA: Open and Efficient Foundation Language Models*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.13971)
document AdamW while pretraining decoder language models from $7$B to $65$B
parameters. Their recipe uses $\beta_1=0.9$, $\beta_2=0.95$, weight decay
$0.1$, gradient clipping, warmup, and cosine learning-rate decay. This chapter
implements the optimizer mechanism, not that full large-scale recipe.

## Train the first cumulative neural language model next

The cumulative training path can now consume each named parameter's
accumulated token-mean gradient, preserve first and second moments across
steps, and commit a fresh zero-gradient leaf. Chapter 23 uses this optimizer to
train a fixed-context embedding-plus-SwiGLU language model and verifies that
its validation loss improves.

AdamW changes parameters; it does not yet define the model whose gradients it
consumes. The next chapter closes that loop while keeping data partitions,
autograd, batching, parameter names, and optimizer state explicit.
