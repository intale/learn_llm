## Freeze the comparison before opening test

Evaluate the frozen validation-selected decoder through one test-only gate,
aggregate every target token fairly, and compare it with a frozen bigram fitted
on the same training tokens. Chapter 33 already chose checkpoint $s^*=8$ with
validation loss $1.595297$ and recorded `test_reads=0` during training and
selection.

Before creating the test gate, the fixture freezes every remaining choice:

1. vocabulary size $V=5$ and context length $T=2$;
2. the Chapter 33 selected parameter snapshot;
3. an add-one bigram fitted on the exact same two training token slices;
4. sequential stride-one windows and batch width $4$; and
5. two test documents of unequal length.

```text
test-a = [4,3,2,1,0,4,3,2,1]
test-b = [3,2,1,0,4,3,2]
```

The first document contributes $14$ aligned target slots and the second
contributes $10$, so predict $N_{te}=24$. The canonical report opens its local
gate once, preserves every decoder parameter and gradient bit, and reports

$$
\mathcal{L}_{te}^{\mathrm{decoder}}=1.607679,qquad
\mathcal{L}_{te}^{\mathrm{bigram}}=2.236735.
$$

Thus the decoder is lower by $\Delta_{te}=0.629055$ in this fixture. The test
documents deliberately reverse the synthetic training cycle, so this result
does not claim that decoders always beat bigrams. It verifies the comparison
mechanics and the reviewed tiny-course acceptance case.

## Average surprise over target tokens

The complete final decoder score is

$$
\mathcal{L}_{te}(\theta_{s^*})=-\frac{1}{N_{te}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{te}}\log p_{\theta_{s^*}}(y_n\mid x_n)
$$

The state $\theta_{s^*}$ is already frozen. Test loss may describe it but cannot
change $s^*$, the learning-rate schedule, the tokenizer, the baseline, or any
other choice.

Unequal documents require token weighting. If document $d$ contributes $N_d$
targets with mean loss $\mathcal{L}^{(d)}_{te}$, then

$$
\mathcal{L}_{te}=\frac{\sum_d N_d\mathcal{L}^{(d)}_{te}}{\sum_d N_d},\qquad N_{te}=\sum_d N_d
$$

For this run, $N_1=14$, $N_2=10$, and $N_{te}=24$. Averaging two document means
would incorrectly give the shorter document the same weight as the longer one.

{/* chapter-section:symbol-glossary */}

## Keep states, slots, and roles distinct

- $\mathcal{L}_{te}$ is token-weighted mean negative log-likelihood on `Test`.
- $\theta_{s^*}$ is the complete decoder state chosen before test opens.
- $s^*$ is the validation-selected checkpoint index, here $s^*=8$.
- $N_{te}$ is the complete test target count, here $N_{te}=24$.
- $n$ indexes one stable document, window, and target position.
- $x_n$ is the causal input available at slot $n$.
- $y_n$ is the observed next-token target at slot $n$.
- $p_{\theta_{s^*}}(y_n\mid x_n)$ is the frozen decoder probability assigned
  to that target.
- $\log$ is the natural logarithm, so loss is in nats per target token.

`Train`, `Validation`, and `Test` are concrete partition variants in the
implementation. Conceptually, training fits, validation selects, and test
supplies final evidence. Once a test result changes a choice, that result has
become selection evidence rather than an independent final measurement.

The local gate is deliberately not a claim of global uniqueness. Another process
could construct another owner from copied data. Real evaluations also require
access control, audit logs, and dataset governance.

## From training scores to governed final LLM evidence

Move from training-set reporting and repeatedly consulted holdouts toward a
frozen three-role protocol: training fits, validation selects, and test supplies
one final comparison. This is a history of LLM evaluation practice, not a history
of programming languages.

[A Neural Probabilistic Language Model](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume3/bengio03a/bengio03a.pdf)
shows an early neural-language-model separation. Bengio and colleagues use
separate training, validation, and test portions, use validation for model
choices and early stopping, and then report test perplexity for an early neural
language model. Training scores
judge data the model already fitted, while repeatedly inspecting one holdout
gradually turns that holdout into another selection signal. The paper supports
the three roles; it does not establish an exactly-once access count.

[Exploring the Limits of Transfer Learning with a Unified Text-to-Text Transformer](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume21/20-074/20-074.pdf)
makes checkpoint responsibility operational at larger scale. Raffel and
colleagues save fine-tuning checkpoints, select by validation performance, and
generally keep exploratory comparisons on validation data to avoid test-set
model selection before final reporting. The selected quantity is task-dependent
validation performance, not universally validation loss.

[Language Models are Few-Shot Learners](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/file/1457c0d6bfcb4967418bfb8ac142f64a-Paper.pdf)
adds the web-scale provenance pressure. Brown and colleagues examine overlap
between web-scale pretraining data and evaluation benchmarks, compare clean
subsets, and omit heavily contaminated language-model tasks, showing why nominal
split labels alone do not settle provenance. A nominal test label therefore does
not by itself prove independence. The mostly small clean-subset changes
permit multiple interpretations and do not prove contamination caused a score
increase.

Neural language-model studies separated fitting, validation-driven choices, and
test reporting; large transfer studies made validation checkpoint choice
operationally explicit, while web-scale pretraining added dataset-overlap and
provenance audits to the evaluation boundary. A trustworthy final LLM comparison
freezes model and data decisions, scores like-for-like targets without a gradient
graph, records provenance, and treats the held-out result as evidence rather than
permission to tune again.

The chapter's one-gate rule is a deliberately strict executable course policy.
It is not a claim that these papers used exactly one test query, and the local
Rust counter is not a replacement for organizational data governance.

## Make the final boundary executable

`EvaluationProvenance` owns nonblank corpus, split, and tokenizer fingerprints
plus a positive context length. Equality is field-by-field, so a matching label
cannot hide a changed tokenizer or context.

`SelectedDecoder` accepts only a `Validation`-selected snapshot.
`FrozenBigram` accepts only a model fitted on `Train`. The Chapter 34 fixture
obtains the exact Chapter 33 training slices through a read-only helper, so the
baseline does not depend on copied constants. It fits the unchanged alpha-one
bigram algorithm before constructing the test evaluator.

`FinalEvaluator` owns a nonempty `Test` epoch and is neither cloneable nor
copyable. Role, provenance, context, and vocabulary errors leave access count
$0$. Immediately before reading test token IDs for scoring, the evaluator sets
the count to $1$. A later error still consumes that local permission.

The decoder is restored from its deep state, scored inside the existing no-grad
boundary, and checked bit for bit afterward. The bigram then visits the exact
same flat input/target slots, including repetitions from overlapping decoder
windows. A length check runs before `zip`, so future alignment drift cannot be
silently truncated.

## Classify legal decisions before you run

1. Two documents contribute $14$ and $10$ targets. Predict the correct
   denominator and explain why the mean of two document means is wrong.
2. A developer lowers the learning rate after seeing test loss. Is that legal
   final reporting or a leak?
3. Another checkpoint has lower test loss than $\theta_{s^*}$. May the report
   replace $s^*$?
4. The decoder scores overlapping context-two windows, but the bigram scores
   each original transition once. Are the resulting means comparable?
5. Change only the tokenizer fingerprint. Does the gate open, and does its
   access count change?
6. Put token ID $5$ into this vocabulary-$5$ test epoch. What happens if the
   error is discovered after test scoring begins?
7. From losses $1.607679$ and $2.236735$, state one justified conclusion and
   one unjustified universal conclusion.
8. Two processes each build a fresh local evaluator over copied test data. What
   external control is missing?

## Carry the selected and evaluated state forward

The cumulative decoder now has one validation-selected state and one immutable
test report on shared targets; Chapter 35 will serialize that exact selected and
evaluated state with its tokenizer, configuration, optimizer, and RNG provenance.

Serialization must reproduce this model and its logits, not reopen test data or
turn the final report into a new selection signal.
