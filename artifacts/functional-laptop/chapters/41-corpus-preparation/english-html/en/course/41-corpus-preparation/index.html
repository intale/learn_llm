Prepare text before the model learns from it

Use NVIDIA NeMo Curator to apply a word-count rule, remove repeated text and preserve known source-group splits, then load prepared JSONL in Rust.

In this chapter

Explain how source choices, exclusions, duplicates and evaluation overlap affect a training corpus; prepare a bounded example with NeMo Curator and load its selected documents through a caller-supplied Rust reader.

Quick reference

Corpus preparation

Terms for selecting training text, handling repeated examples, protecting evaluation roles and reading prepared documents.

Decontamination
Check and exclude training overlap with declared protected evaluation material using a specified method. Passing that check does not establish absence of every possible leak.
Document occurrence
One stored instance of a document. Two records containing the same text are two occurrences and can give that text extra training weight.
Exact deduplication
Remove repeated text under a declared equality policy. The NeMo example groups text hashes and keeps one representative; it does not detect paraphrases or certify collision-proof equality.
Held-out split
Validation or test documents kept out of tokenizer learning and model fitting. Validation informs setting choices; test evaluation is reserved for the final assessment.
Near-duplicate
A document sufficiently similar to another under a declared matching method. Approximate searches can miss related text or group text that should remain distinct.
Prepared-document interchange
UTF-8 JSONL records with nonblank string id and text fields and retained metadata. Rust loads the supplied selection without redoing filtering, duplicate removal or split assignment.
Retention fraction
Prepared-document count divided by input-document count for a nonempty input. It measures how much the declared pipeline retains, not the quality of its survivors.
Source group
Records declared to belong to one related source family. The exercise assigns each supplied group to a single split; that assignment does not prove all related records were discovered.
Training corpus
The selected documents that supply examples for tokenizer learning and model training, together with evidence of their source and preparation policy.

The problem: the model learns from the text we let through

A language model learns to predict tokens from the documents supplied during training. Its optimizer does not decide whether those documents are appropriate for the task, whether we may use them, or whether a purportedly unseen test example also appears in the training input. Those decisions must happen before training.

Repeated text creates another problem. If one training pass visits every stored document occurrence, each copy of a document supplies another copy of its token targets. The repeated text receives more influence without adding new examples. Removing duplicates changes that weighting; it does not merely save disk space.

Corpus preparation therefore has two outputs: selected text and evidence about how it was selected. We need source context, declared exclusion rules, duplicate handling and protected evaluation boundaries. A large file or a successful download supplies none of those conclusions on its own.

We will use NVIDIA NeMo Curator to prepare text. It is an external tool, not part of the Rust language model, and another preparation tool can replace it if it produces the same documented input for the model. Rust will only load the prepared documents. The tokenizer in Chapter 42 will then learn from the training documents, not the validation or test documents.

The solution: prepare documents, then load a stable format

Keep one document as one record throughout the preparation pipeline. Retain a stable document ID and the source information needed to trace that record. A source record should identify where the text came from, its revision and the applicable license and attribution. A checksum identifies selected bytes; it does not establish permission to use those bytes or tell us what they contain.

Apply declared quality rules, inspect what those rules exclude, and handle duplicates before final training input is frozen. Quality rules are proxies: for example, a minimum word count rejects short records, not every unsuitable record. A rule can also remove useful text. Inspect both survivors and exclusions rather than interpreting a low exclusion rate as success.

Keep privacy review separate from quality scoring. Passing a word-count filter or removing duplicates does not remove names, addresses, credentials or other sensitive content. Withhold unresolved records from training; do not copy their sensitive text into public reports. Retain enough source and transformation information to identify affected derived corpora if an input is withdrawn. Removing a source from a later corpus does not erase what an already trained model may have learned from it.

A six-document example

The practical example uses course-authored text, not a result for a large training corpus. Its JSONL input contains these six documents:

Document IDTextSource groupRelevant observation
story-aA cat naps.family-aThree words
story-bA cat naps.family-aAn identical copy of story-a
story-cA dog runs.family-bThree words
story-dRain feeds trees.family-cThree words
story-ehifamily-eOne word
story-f!!!family-fPunctuation only

Configure NeMo’s word-count filter to retain records with at least 333 and at most 202020 words. Its English setting counts whitespace-separated items; that proxy is not a linguistic definition of a word. The 444 story occurrences survive; hi and the punctuation-only record do not. Exact duplicate removal then leaves 333 distinct story texts. The punctuation-only record has one whitespace-separated item too; this particular rule rejects it for length, not because it recognizes punctuation as unsuitable language-model training text. The duplicate-removal workflow may retain either cat-story ID; the result we care about here is one cat-story occurrence, not a promised representative ID or output-file order.

Before deduplication, the cat story occupies 222 of the 444 surviving document occurrences. After deduplication, it occupies 111 of 333 distinct documents. Under uniform sampling of these whole-stage document occurrences before separating their roles, its selection probability would change from 2/42/42/4 to 1/31/31/3. That calculation illustrates duplicate weighting, not a sampler used to train on this fixture. Actual token-target weighting also depends on document length, split selection and the batching policy; document fractions are not token fractions.

NeMo’s exact method groups hashes of text and identifies copies to remove. It does not identify paraphrases, and hash grouping is not an adversarial collision-proof comparison. NeMo’s fuzzy method uses approximate similarity search for near-duplicates. Its configuration trades missed matches against unwanted matches; it does not certify a universal similarity cutoff or detect all ways an evaluation example could have leaked into training.

Freeze the roles of the prepared documents

The training split supplies examples for fitting the tokenizer and model. The validation split supplies examples for selecting settings or deciding when to stop training. The test split is reserved for final evaluation. Do not move a difficult held-out document into training after seeing its score.

Related records must not be scattered across those roles. Treat an identified duplicate family or other known related source group as one unit when assigning splits. Otherwise two different IDs can put substantially the same example in both training and evaluation. For this fixture, the two cat-story copies share family-a; their different IDs do not make them independent examples.

For the exercise, a supplied policy assigns family-a, family-e and family-f to training, family-b to validation and family-c to test. We fix that policy before running the filters. Both cat-story copies therefore have the same role regardless of which ID duplicate removal keeps. The prepared fixture has one document in each role: the cat story in training, the dog story in validation and the rain story in test.

The recipe uses Pandas to join those declared assignments to the source-group metadata. It refuses an unassigned group rather than choosing a convenient role after seeing the output. This checks the supplied groups, not whether we have discovered every related pair. One held-out story is also far too little evidence for a meaningful model-quality estimate.

Split disjointness and decontamination are different checks. Disjoint IDs do not prove different text. Removing exact duplicates does not prove that near-copies or external benchmark examples are absent. Declare the protected evaluation material and the overlap method before inspecting results, report its measured scope, and keep unresolved overlaps out of training. A check against a named evaluation set does not establish internet-wide contamination freedom.

Export documents, not preparation-specific objects

The handoff to Rust is UTF-8 JSON Lines: one JSON object per physical line. Each object has a nonblank string id and a nonblank string text. Other fields are retained as metadata. For example:

{"id":"story-c","text":"A dog runs.","source_group":"family-b"}

The document ID is a label, not a token ID or a required filename. The text is still text; tokenization comes next. A newline inside document text must be escaped as \n inside the JSON string, so it does not create a second record. Source and split records remain linked to the exported documents, but a JSONL file alone does not certify that those decisions were made correctly.

Formula: distinguish retention from quality

For a nonempty input corpus, the final document-retention fraction is

rkeep=NpreparedNinputr_{\mathrm{keep}}=\frac{N_{\mathrm{prepared}}}{N_{\mathrm{input}}}rkeep​=Ninput​Nprepared​​

Here NinputN_{\mathrm{input}}Ninput​ is the number of input document occurrences, NpreparedN_{\mathrm{prepared}}Nprepared​ is the number remaining after the declared preparation stages, and rkeepr_{\mathrm{keep}}rkeep​ is a dimensionless fraction. Neither count is a byte count or token count. With zero input documents, the fraction is undefined; report that condition rather than inventing a zero-quality score.

In the six-document example, quality filtering retains 4/64/64/6 of the original input. Duplicate removal retains 3/43/43/4 of its own input: the four records that reached that stage. Their product is the final retention fraction,

46⋅34=36=12.\frac{4}{6}\cdot\frac{3}{4}=\frac{3}{6}=\frac{1}{2}.64​⋅43​=63​=21​.

Changing the denominator changes the question. Reporting 1/61/61/6 as the duplicate stage’s exclusion fraction would use the original input population rather than the 444 records the duplicate stage examined. Keep every stage’s entering and leaving document counts so its fraction can be interpreted locally.

Keeping half the input says how much remains under this policy. It does not say that half was bad, that the survivors are safe, or that a model trained on them will perform well. Those claims need different evidence.

Practical preparation with NeMo Curator

This workflow uses a separately built NeMo Curator image. You need Linux, Docker, a compatible NVIDIA GPU and driver, and the NVIDIA Container Toolkit so Docker can expose the GPU. The pinned NeMo release must be compatible with that CUDA/driver stack. Rust’s prepared-corpus reader does not require a GPU or CUDA.

Run the following commands from the repository root. The image build may use network access to obtain the external tool. It does not acquire a training corpus or add NeMo to the Rust/site image:

docker build --file docker/nemo-curator.Dockerfile \
  --tag learn-llm-nemo-curator:26.07 .

Create a fresh output directory for the exercise, then run the tool with network access disabled. The read-only mount supplies the recipe and its six-record fixture; the writable mount receives generated preparation output:

mkdir -p .build/corpus/ch41-exact-fixture
docker run --rm --network none --gpus all --memory=8g --cpus=2 --shm-size=1g \
  --hostname course-nemo --add-host course-nemo:127.0.0.1 \
  --mount type=bind,source="$PWD/tools/nemo-curator",target=/course,readonly \
  --mount type=bind,source="$PWD/.build/corpus/ch41-exact-fixture",target=/output \
  learn-llm-nemo-curator:26.07 \
  /course/prepare_corpus.py \
    --input /course/fixtures/raw.jsonl --output /output \
    --split-policy /course/fixtures/splits.json

Use an empty output directory. If the directory already contains an earlier attempt, choose a different directory rather than overwriting its evidence. The hostname mapping lets the local Ray workers resolve their container on loopback; it does not enable external network access.

The recipe invokes NeMo’s reader, configured word-count filter, exact duplicate identification and duplicate-removal workflow. Identification produces IDs to remove; it is not already the prepared text. The removal workflow must consume those IDs from the same input and ID policy, then export the remaining documents.

The filter configuration in tools/nemo-curator/prepare_corpus.py uses the library’s pipeline and stages; the recipe does not implement its own filter:

Pipeline(
    name="course_corpus_quality",
    stages=[
        JsonlReader(file_paths=str(input_path), read_kwargs=read_kwargs),
        ScoreFilter(
            filter_obj=WordCountFilter(min_words=min_words, max_words=max_words, lang="en"),
            score_field="word_count",
        ),
        JsonlWriter(path=str(quality_path), mode="error"),
    ],
).run(executor=executor)

The surrounding recipe supplies the reader settings, starts a local Ray client, and runs NeMo’s exact-identification and text-removal workflows. The defaults set min_words to 3 and max_words to 20. Command-line options change those values; retain them with the output report when comparing different policies. The supplied split-policy file is independent of the observed scores.

Inspect the generated report and exported files with these commands, still from the repository root:

cat .build/corpus/ch41-exact-fixture/report.json
cat .build/corpus/ch41-exact-fixture/prepared.jsonl
cat .build/corpus/ch41-exact-fixture/train.jsonl

For this fixture, the expected stage counts are 666 input documents, 444 after word-count filtering and 333 after exact duplicate removal. The final texts are A cat naps., A dog runs. and Rain feeds trees.; do not require one particular cat-story ID. The report also records one document in each role. prepared.jsonl contains all three documents for inspection; train.jsonl, validation.jsonl and test.jsonl contain their respective selections and retain the split metadata.

This is a tiny execution example with a fixed assignment of known source groups. It is not an approval of a larger corpus, a privacy check, proof that all related groups were found or a trained-model result. The helper limits input to 444 MiB and 409640964096 documents by default so the example is inspectable; those are not a recipe for processing the previously acquired large corpus. A bulk run needs its own feasible configuration and complete selection evidence.

For a real corpus, also retain input provenance, the tool version and configuration, excluded-document counts, the duplicate policy, split assignments and the scope of overlap and privacy review. A successful small fixture does not establish that the same resource settings fit a larger dataset. Select and measure that workload separately, preserving the raw inputs and every failed attempt.

Load the prepared output in Rust

PreparedCorpusReader accepts any caller-supplied BufRead stream. A file, memory buffer or database export can supply that stream without changing the loader. Standard buffered I/O reads physical lines; Serde JSON parses their objects. The loader preserves decoded text and metadata. It does not normalize text, reject duplicate content or assign a split.

The caller chooses maximum source-record bytes, document count and cumulative decoded-text bytes. Record bytes include the physical line ending; decoded text bytes count the UTF-8 representation of the text value, not JSON syntax or metadata. An oversized record, malformed JSON, missing required field, failed read or exceeded limit stops the stream with an error. No failed document is returned or counted. Documents returned earlier are not rolled back.

The demo reads standard input, retains only the first three document descriptions for its summary, and emits that summary only after the complete input succeeds. It can therefore inspect a large stream without retaining every text. This is not a transactional storage adapter: a caller storing documents as they arrive must provide its own staging if partial results must stay unpublished.

With Docker running and the Rust workspace image available, load the output from the preceding NeMo command. Run from the repository root:

COURSE_CORPUS=false ./course run cargo run --offline --locked \
  -p ch41-corpus-preparation \
  < .build/corpus/ch41-exact-fixture/prepared.jsonl

The Rust summary should report 333 loaded documents and 393939 decoded text bytes. Those bytes are 111111 for the cat story, 111111 for the dog story and 171717 for the rain story. Metadata and JSON punctuation are not included in that sum. The wrapper runs the Rust program without network access; COURSE_CORPUS=false avoids acquiring the optional large corpus while preparing the Rust image.

For the tokenizer’s training input, select the training export instead of the all-role inspection file:

COURSE_CORPUS=false ./course run cargo run --offline --locked \
-p ch41-corpus-preparation \
  < .build/corpus/ch41-exact-fixture/train.jsonl

This summary reports 111 document and 111111 decoded text bytes. Choosing that file is the caller’s responsibility: the loader does not infer a role from a filename or reject held-out records automatically.

History: more text was not the whole solution

Collecting readable text alone does not document its intended use, control the weight of repeated occurrences or protect held-out evaluation from training overlap.

Dataset documentation makes source context reviewable; deduplication studies connect repeated training text with memorization and train-test overlap in their measured settings.

Datasheets for Datasets was first posted in 201820182018. Gebru and colleagues propose documenting dataset motivation, composition, collection and intended uses so those choices can be reviewed. Documentation records context; it does not itself establish permission or privacy.

The 202120212021 C4 analysis examined where web-corpus text came from and what filtering removed. Its finding that blocklist filtering disproportionately excluded text from and about minority individuals is a reason to inspect exclusions, not to label every filter neutral.

In 202220222022, Deduplicating Training Data Makes Language Models Better studied duplicate handling in training corpora. Lee and colleagues report reduced memorized output and train-test overlap after deduplication in their studied corpora and models; those outcomes are not measurements of this course fixture. They explain why duplicate handling and evaluation separation matter in addition to collecting and documenting text.

Follow the preparation-to-learning boundary

The preparation tool owns the configured exclusions and duplicate handling. The exported documents and their source and split evidence cross the boundary into the Rust training pipeline. The Rust loader reads that selection; the tokenizer learns only from the training role. Passing a record between stages does not grant it a new quality or privacy guarantee.

Explanations for the prepared-corpus loading tasks

  1. The manually prepared fixture has its own origin. Matching its document and byte counts to the NeMo example checks only those quantities, not its producer, selection policy, source rights, privacy or split assignment. Keep the actual NeMo input, configuration and output evidence when reproducing that workflow.

  2. For malformed later input, the iterator has already returned the earlier valid document before it encounters the error. The demo withholds its final success summary because it consumes the stream completely before printing. A different caller that wrote each returned document immediately would need its own staging to prevent partial publication.

What reaches the language model next

Prepared training documents become the tokenizer’s learning input in Chapter 42. Held-out documents may be encoded with the learned tokenizer, but they must not change its learned vocabulary or merge choices. Later batching creates token targets from that same frozen training selection. NeMo has not implemented any of those Rust LLM algorithms; it has supplied documented text for them to learn from.
