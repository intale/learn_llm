History: reporting conditions and measuring variation A language-model score without its input conditions and scope does not identify what was evaluated or establish which capabilities were checked. Model Cards for Model Reporting was presented in 2019. Mitchell and colleagues propose model documentation that includes intended uses and evaluation conditions; a model card is not itself proof of quality. Model Cards describe evaluation conditions and intended uses; later fine-tuning experiments study variation across initializations and data orders. Reporting scope and measuring variation answer different questions. In Fine-Tuning Pretrained Language Models: Weight Initializations, Data Orders, and Early Stopping, published in 2020, Dodge and colleagues study fine-tuning variability associated with initialization and data order for pretrained language models. This does not measure variability in the course’s tiny decoder.
