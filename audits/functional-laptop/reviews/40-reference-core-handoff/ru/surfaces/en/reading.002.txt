What the reference actually does The fixture assigns 8 documents to training, 2 to validation and 2 to test. The decoder has 1188 learned scalar parameters and context capacity 4 tokens. Its primary training schedule and separately executed replay each perform 32 updates on full batches of 16 windows, with 4 valid targets per window. Therefore, each schedule consumes 32\cdot16\cdot4=2048 valid training targets. One pipeline invocation includes both schedules: 4096 targets altogether. These counts are derived from the frozen full-batch schedule, not from a new runtime token counter. Evaluation targets and generated tokens are separate quantities. The report’s independent_schedules field counts two separately executed copies of the same schedule from training seed 39, not statistically independent seed samples. The replay agrees bit for bit with the primary training result in this execution. The selected decoder and frozen bigram then score the same 1744 overlapping test-window target slots. Their mean negative log-likelihoods are approximately 3.866087547 and 3.981342714 nats per slot, respectively. This known comparison is a regression check: rerunning it can expose changes in the checked behavior. It is not an independent estimate of generalization. There are only 442 within-document transition occurrences in those test documents. Overlapping windows repeat occurrences, so 1744 is not a count of distinct transitions. A separate policy would score each occurrence once, using the longest available causal prefix capped at four tokens and only its newest-position distribution. This run does not report numeric mean NLL or perplexity under that policy. The checkpoint round trip preserves its encoded bytes, model and optimizer bits, and tokenizer.
