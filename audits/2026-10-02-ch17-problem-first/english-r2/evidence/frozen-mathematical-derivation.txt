# Mathematical evidence and limits

This is a derivation, not a device or trained-model observation.

For a dense linear output, y_j = sum_i x_i W_ij. With independent, zero-centered
weights, independent zero-centered input terms of common variance, and weight/input
independence, cross terms vanish. Thus Var(y_j) = fan_in Var(W_ij) Var(x_i).
Under analogous backward independence and zero-centered arriving-gradient
assumptions with common gradient variance, the fan_out factor
controls propagated gradient variance. Unit forward and backward multipliers
ask respectively for fan_in Var(W_ij)=1 and fan_out Var(W_ij)=1. These constraints
coincide at equal widths; 2/(fan_in+fan_out) is the stated compromise otherwise.
Near-linear, symmetric activation behavior motivates applying this model; it
does not exactly describe a SiLU/residual/RMSNorm decoder or trained dependent
weights.

A zero-centered real uniform distribution on [-a,a] has second moment a^2/3.
Equating that moment to the target gives a=sqrt(6/(fan_in+fan_out)). Endpoint
closure does not affect the ideal continuous distribution; the actual course
sampler maps a finite binary64 draw grid to a represented interval [-a,a).
A finite matrix need not have exactly the distribution's target variance.
Fans 2+2 give variance 1/2, SD 1/sqrt(2), bound sqrt(3/2). Fans 4+2 give variance
1/3, SD 1/sqrt(3), bound 1. Doubling a uniform bound multiplies distribution
variance by four; four independent equal-width linear layers compound that
multiplier per layer, not as a measured decoder claim.

The tiny zero-symmetry fixture has x=[1,-1], zero weights, SiLU derivative 1/2
at zero, and equal outgoing weights. Both input-weight gradient columns are
[0.5,-0.5]^T. Equal parameter treatment preserves their equality; nonzero
gradients do not alone break symmetry. This is one stated path, not a theorem
that every zero-initialized parameter is unusable.
