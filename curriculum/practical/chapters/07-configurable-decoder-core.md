## Formula

Compute exact $d_h=D/H_q$ first; require even $d_h$ and exact
$H_q/H_{kv}$ with $H_{kv}\le H_q$. Set $K=H_{kv}d_h$.
Unique learned scalars satisfy
$P=VD+L(2D^2+2DK+3DF+2D)+D$.
Count the embedding once; the transpose head aliases it. Query/output contribute
$2D^2$ per block, key/value $2DK$, SwiGLU matrices $3DF$, block gains $2D$,
and the final gain $D$. RoPE contributes no learned position table; no biases.

For full-context physical KV capacity use $2BLC Kb_{kv}$ bytes. Logical prefix
length and reset do not change allocated capacity. Two-byte comparison values
are $1024$, $16384$, $2097152$, $10737418240$ bytes. Eight-byte `f64`
accounting at nominal rows gives $4096$ and $65536$ reference/bridge bytes;
the existing cache actually supports one row and owns $256$ and $8192$ payload
bytes at physical capacities four and sixteen, before overhead. Production BF16 KV alone exceeds the laptop
$6710886400$-byte device envelope by $4026531840$ bytes.

For one nominal full-context unsegmented forward, linear MACs are
$BC[L(2D^2+2DK+3DF)+DV]$. Add $BLDC(C+1)$ for causal triangular score/mix
MACs or $2BLDC^2$ for dense materialized score/mix MACs. Exact causal/dense
series are $76544/77312$, $1122304/1183744$,
$17718837248/18790481920$ and $2981072375644160/3684738342584320$.
A MAC is one multiply-accumulate pair, not a complete FLOP count or measured
latency. Norm/RoPE/softmax/activation/masking scalar work is excluded. Packed
segment edges and tiled execution need their own separate counts; $N$ is not
a tensor axis or an automatic multiplier of the forward estimate.

State is the sum of per-buffer aligned allocations; declare each owner's element
count, dtype width, alignment and alias. Count a master only when separately
allocated. The illustrative widths $2/4/4/4/4$ for weight/master/gradient/two
moments give $18P$ unrounded, or $1251016851456$ production bytes. This is
an accounting example, distinct from the actual scalar `f64` layout. The selected
descriptor also enumerates nonlearned RoPE inverse-frequency, cosine and sine
tables as persistent owners. CPU tables use `f64`; non-CPU symbolic phase tables
use four-byte elements separately from two-byte weight accounting. Per-rank
state partitions only declared matrix axes and retains replicated norm gains;
these nonparameter tables do not change $P$ or the global unrounded $18P$ example.

Generate checked alloc/release/alias/workspace events for the selected schedule.
Take the maximum simultaneous sum, including persistent state and KV; do not
add independent maxima. Persistent $64$, A $64$, B $128$ and B-workspace $32$
bytes give $288$ when A overlaps B, or $224$ after A's release. Aliasing B
adds no allocation. These synthetic event fixtures do not measure decoder RSS.
The complete symbolic schedule covers residual/norm/QKV/RoPE/score/weight/
weighted-head/output/FFN/gain-stat/logit/target buffers and their adjoints when
tracked. Untracked full retains inspectable primal `BlockForward` tensors until
its result is dropped, omitting graph/adjoints. Cached one-row accounting
conservatively reserves staged tickets until commit and physical KV capacity.
Pure resident peak and the lower bound including known event workspace are
separate. Complete combined peak is absent whenever required workspace is
Unknown; unknown actual GPU workspace prevents readiness.

TP8 forward traffic is sent bytes, separate from resident storage. A residual
payload has $32768\cdot8192=268435456$ elements, partitioned into eight
$33554432$-element chunks. Reduce-scatter plus all-gather sends $14$ chunks
per rank/event; two events/block over $80$ blocks give $160$ events. At two
bytes/element: $939524096$ bytes/rank/event, $150323855360$ bytes/rank,
$1202590842880$ summed across ranks. Backward, optimizer and protocol traffic
are excluded; no communication is executed. Matrix shards follow exact declared
axes and norms remain replicated. Single-device traffic has zero events.
